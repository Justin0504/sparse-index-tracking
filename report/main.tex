\begin{abstract}
% ============================================================
The covariance matrix used to construct a sparse index-tracking portfolio is typically estimated by minimizing prediction error---yet the portfolio depends on only the $K \times K$ sub-matrix of the $K$ selected assets, not the full $N \times N$ estimate. We show that this mismatch is costly and quantifiable: the cosine similarity between the prediction-loss and tracking-error gradients is bounded by $\sqrt{2K/N}$, so most estimation effort is wasted on decision-irrelevant entries. Training the covariance model end-to-end through a differentiable quadratic program eliminates this waste, reducing annualized tracking error by 5--12\% ($p = 0.002$) on S\&P~500 data spanning 19 years and nine rolling folds. For single-parameter shrinkage models, a grid search over the tracking-error objective achieves the same gain without differentiable optimization; for richer models (12+ parameters), differentiable optimization is the only viable training path, with significant improvements confirmed at $N{=}478$ ($p = 0.031$). The improvement is robust to position-size limits, transaction costs, turnover penalties, and market regime changes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

This project investigates a specific instance of the \emph{predict-then-optimize gap} studied throughout CSCI 619: when the prediction loss used to train a model is misaligned with the downstream decision loss, end-to-end training can improve decision quality. We identify a financial setting---sparse index tracking---where this gap is \textbf{structurally amplified by the sparsity constraint}, and where the degree of amplification is \textbf{quantitatively predictable from problem parameters alone}.

\paragraph{Sparse index tracking.}
Index tracking replicates a benchmark (e.g., S\&P 500) using only $K \ll N$ stocks to reduce transaction costs and management overhead. The standard approach estimates the asset covariance matrix $\Sigma$ from data, then solves a quadratic program (QP) to find weights minimizing tracking error. This two-stage pipeline treats all $N^2$ covariance entries equally during estimation---but the QP solution depends on only the $K \times K$ sub-matrix of selected stocks.

\paragraph{The structural gap.}
At $K=20$, $N=100$: the QP uses 400 entries (4\% of the matrix), yet MSE optimizes all 10{,}000. We prove that the cosine similarity between MSE and tracking-error gradients is bounded by $\sqrt{2K/N}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment}), meaning at $K/N = 0.05$, at most 32\% of the prediction gradient aligns with the tracking-error direction. This bound makes a testable prediction: DFL should help most when $K/N$ is small.

\paragraph{Why this problem for DFL.}
We chose sparse index tracking as our testbed for three structural reasons:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Tunable misalignment.} $K/N$ directly controls how much of $\Sigma$ the decision uses. We can dial $K$ and watch the DFL advantage appear, peak, and vanish.
  \item \textbf{Hard zero boundary.} Entries outside $\cS \times \cS$ have \emph{exactly zero} influence on the portfolio---not diminishing influence, but a hard zero. This creates maximum misalignment.
  \item \textbf{Clean QP.} The optimization is a standard convex QP, differentiable via KKT conditions without approximations.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Objective alignment drives the improvement.} Switching from prediction-error to tracking-error objectives reduces TE by 5--12\% ($p = 0.002$). The mechanism is \emph{alignment}, not differentiable optimization per se: a grid search over validation tracking error achieves the same gain for simple models.
  \item \textbf{Gradient alignment bound.} We prove $\cos(\mathbf{g}_{\text{MSE}}, \mathbf{g}_{\text{task}}) \leq \sqrt{2K/N}$, formalizing why misalignment is worst for sparse portfolios, and validate it empirically.
  \item \textbf{DFL enables richer models.} For covariance models with 12+ parameters (per-sector shrinkage targets, regime-adaptive shrinkage), differentiable optimization is the only viable training path, achieving significant gains at $N{=}478$ ($p = 0.031$).
  \item \textbf{Robustness.} The improvement survives UCITS position limits, transaction costs (10--100 bps), turnover penalties, and diverse market regimes without increasing portfolio concentration.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Decision Problem}
\label{sec:formulation}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Tracking Error and the QP}

Let $\br_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote asset returns, $r_t^{\text{idx}}$ the index return, and $\bw \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the portfolio weights. The expected squared tracking error is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\text{TE}_t(\bw)^2] = \bw^\top \Sigma \bw - 2\bw^\top \bsigma_{\text{idx}} + \sigma_{\text{idx}}^2,
  \label{eq:te}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma = \text{Cov}(\br_t)$ and $\bsigma_{\text{idx}} = \Sigma \bw_{\text{idx}}$ for known index weights $\bw_{\text{idx}}$.

\subsection{Hard-$K$ Formulation}

Select a subset $\cS \subseteq \{1,\dots,N\}$ with $|\cS| = K$, then solve:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\bw_\cS} \; \bw_\cS^\top \bSigma_{\cS\cS} \bw_\cS - 2\bw_\cS^\top \hat{\bsigma}_{\text{idx},\cS}
  \quad \text{s.t.} \; \mathbf{1}^\top \bw_\cS = 1, \; \bw_\cS \geq 0.
  \label{eq:hard-k}
\end{equation}

The key elements of this decision problem:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Decision variable:} $\bw_\cS \in \mathbb{R}^K$ (portfolio weights over selected stocks)
  \item \textbf{Objective:} minimize expected squared tracking error
  \item \textbf{Constraints:} fully invested ($\mathbf{1}^\top \bw = 1$), long-only ($\bw \geq 0$)
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty:} $\Sigma$ is unknown; must be estimated from historical return data
  \item \textbf{Timing:} observe features $\to$ estimate $\bSigma$ $\to$ solve QP $\to$ hold portfolio $\to$ observe realized TE
  \item \textbf{What is estimated:} only $\bSigma_{\cS\cS}$ and $\hat{\bsigma}_{\text{idx},\cS}$ enter the QP---the remaining $(N{-}K)^2$ entries of $\bSigma$ have zero influence on the decision
\end{itemize}

This final point is the structural observation that motivates our entire project: \textbf{the prediction model outputs $N^2$ entries, but the decision uses only $\sim K^2$}.

\section{Why Sparsity Amplifies the Gap}
\label{sec:theory}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Gradient Analysis}

The task gradient decomposes via the chain rule:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\theta \ell = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \ell}{\partial \bw^*}}_{\text{task}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial \bw^*}{\partial \bSigma}}_{\text{QP Jacobian}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial \bSigma}{\partial \theta}}_{\text{model}}.
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}

The QP Jacobian $\partial \bw^* / \partial \bSigma$, derived from KKT conditions \citep{amos2017optnet}, acts as a \emph{sparsity-aware projection}: it zeroes out gradient contributions from all entries $\bSigma_{ij}$ where $i \notin \cS$ or $j \notin \cS$. In contrast, the MSE gradient distributes signal uniformly across all $N^2$ entries.

\subsection{Gradient Alignment Bound}

\begin{proposition}[Gradient Alignment Bound]
\label{prop:alignment}
Let $\cS \subset \{1,\ldots,N\}$ with $|\cS| = K$. Define the task-relevant support $\mathcal{T} = \{(i,j) : i \in \cS \text{ or } j \in \cS\}$. The task gradient satisfies $(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{task}})_{ij} = 0$ for all $(i,j) \notin \mathcal{T}$. Under isotropic MSE gradient energy:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\cos(\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{MSE}},\, \mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{task}})\right] \leq \sqrt{\frac{2K}{N} - \frac{K^2}{N^2}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2K}{N}} \quad \text{for } K \ll N.
\label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\textbf{Proof sketch.} (i) KKT differentiation shows the task gradient is zero outside $\mathcal{T}$; (ii) Cauchy--Schwarz on the restricted inner product; (iii) isotropy gives the energy ratio $|\mathcal{T}|/N^2 = 2K/N - K^2/N^2$.

\subsection{Empirical Gradient Attribution}

We compute the task gradient $\partial \ell / \partial \bSigma_{ij}$ numerically at 10 dates ($K{=}20$, $N{=}97$). Of the total task gradient energy, $87.5\% \pm 2.8\%$ is concentrated in the $K \times K$ block, with only 12.5\% in the cross block and zero outside. Under MSE's isotropy, only $K^2/N^2 = 4.3\%$ falls in the selected block. The $20\times$ concentration factor confirms the QP Jacobian acts as a strong sparsity-aware filter.

\section{Solution: Two Paths to Alignment}
\label{sec:method}
% ============================================================

The mechanism behind DFL's improvement is \emph{objective alignment}: training the covariance model to minimize tracking error instead of prediction error. We identify two paths to achieve this.

\subsection{Path 1: Grid Search (Val-Tuned)}

For single-parameter models, enumerate candidate values on validation tracking error:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha^* = \arg\min_{\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.02, \ldots, 1.0\}} \text{TE}_{\text{val}}(\bw^*(\bSigma(\alpha)))
\end{equation}
This requires no differentiable optimization---just 50 forward QP solves (0.6 seconds).

\subsection{Path 2: Differentiable QP (End-to-End)}

For multi-parameter models, train through the QP via backpropagation:
\begin{equation}
  \cL_{\text{task}} = \frac{252}{H} \sum_{s=1}^{H} \bigl(\bw^*(\bSigma_t)^\top \br_{t+s} - r_{t+s}^{\text{idx}}\bigr)^2 + \gamma \|\bw^*_t - \bw^*_{t-1}\|_1,
  \label{eq:task}
\end{equation}
where $H=21$ trading days, 252 annualizes, and $\gamma$ controls turnover regularization. Gradients flow through KKT conditions of the QP \citep{agrawal2019differentiable} into model parameters $\theta$.

Stock selection $\cS$ is \emph{detached} from the computational graph. We use market-cap-based selection (top-$K$ by index weight), which is deterministic and stable. Making selection differentiable via relaxation introduces training instability and excessive turnover---we validate this empirically in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.

\subsection{The Key Distinction}

The contribution is \textbf{not} ``DFL beats two-stage.'' It is a decomposition:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Objective alignment} is the mechanism---any method achieving alignment (grid search, SPO+, DFL) closes the gap equally for simple models.
  \item \textbf{Differentiable optimization} is a scalable means---it becomes essential when the model has too many parameters for exhaustive search.
\end{enumerate}
These two components are empirically separable, as we show in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Training Procedure}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Task-Aware Covariance Estimation for Sparse Index Tracking}
\label{alg:dfl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Covariance model $f_\theta$, index weights $\bw_{\text{idx}}$, cardinality $K$, training returns $\br_{1:T}$, rebalancing dates $\{t_1, \dots, t_M\}$
\FOR{epoch $= 1, \dots, E$}
  \FOR{each rebalancing date $t_j$}
    \STATE Compute features $\bx_{t_j}$ from $\br_{1:t_j}$
    \STATE Predict covariance: $\bSigma_{t_j} \leftarrow f_\theta(\bx_{t_j})$
    \STATE Select top-$K$ stocks: $\cS \leftarrow \text{TopK}(\bw_{\text{idx}}, K)$ \hfill {\footnotesize\textit{(detached from gradient)}}
    \STATE Solve differentiable QP: $\bw^*_{t_j} \leftarrow \arg\min_{\bw_\cS} \bw_\cS^\top \bSigma_{\cS\cS} \bw_\cS - 2\bw_\cS^\top \hat{\bsigma}_{\text{idx},\cS}$
    \STATE Compute realized TE over forward window: $\cL_{\text{task}} \leftarrow \frac{252}{H}\sum_{s=1}^{H}(\bw^{*\top}_{t_j} \br_{t_j+s} - r^{\text{idx}}_{t_j+s})^2$
    \STATE Backpropagate $\nabla_\theta \cL_{\text{task}}$ through QP via KKT
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE Update $\theta$ with accumulated gradients (Adam, lr=$10^{-3}$, cosine annealing)
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

In the two-stage baseline, line~7 is replaced by $\cL_{\text{MSE}} = \|\bSigma - \Sigma_{\text{realized}}\|_F^2$, and the QP is solved only at test time.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Data and Evaluation Protocol}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Universe:} S\&P 500 top 100 by market cap ($N{=}97$ after filtering for $\geq$90\% coverage)
  \item \textbf{Period:} 2006--2025, daily returns (19 years, 4{,}800+ trading days)
  \item \textbf{Features:} 21-day realized volatility, 63-day momentum, GICS sector (one-hot), market-cap quintile
  \item \textbf{Benchmark:} Cap-weighted index of same 100 stocks
  \item \textbf{Protocol:} Rolling window: 3-year train, 6-month validation, 1-year test, rolling forward 1 year $\to$ \textbf{9 folds}
  \item \textbf{Sparsity:} $K \in \{5, 10, 20, 30, 50\}$
  \item \textbf{Statistics:} Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-parametric, appropriate for 9 folds), 10{,}000-sample bootstrap CIs
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baselines}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Naive market-cap:} top-$K$ by index weight, no QP. Lower bound on what optimization adds.
  \item \textbf{Ledoit-Wolf (2004) + QP:} linear shrinkage, analytical $\alpha$ minimizing Frobenius loss.
  \item \textbf{Ledoit-Wolf (2020) + QP:} nonlinear shrinkage via Stieltjes transform. Statistical SOTA for covariance estimation.
  \item \textbf{Val-Tuned shrinkage:} grid search over 50 $\alpha$ values on validation TE. No differentiable QP. Isolates the value of objective alignment from the DFL machinery.
  \item \textbf{SPO+ surrogate \citep{elmachtoub2022smart}:} grid search minimizing SPO+ decision regret.
  \item \textbf{Random search:} 200--500 random configurations for multi-parameter models (strongest black-box alternative to DFL).
  \item \textbf{Direct weight MLP:} bypass QP entirely, predict weights directly. Tests the value of QP structure.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Three Experimental Questions}

Our experiments are organized around three specific questions, each testing a distinct aspect of the predict-then-optimize gap:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item[\textbf{Q1.}] \textbf{Does objective alignment improve tracking?} (Main effect)
  \item[\textbf{Q2.}] \textbf{Is DFL necessary, or does grid search suffice?} (Mechanism decomposition)
  \item[\textbf{Q3.}] \textbf{When does DFL become essential?} (Boundary condition for DFL's unique value)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Additional Checks and Computational Cost}

Further robustness checks confirm stability: lookback windows (21--126 days) yield gains of 10--12\%; rebalancing frequency (weekly/biweekly/monthly) shows stable $\sim$12\% gain; cross-benchmark tests (Russell 2000, Tech sector) replicate 1-param findings; and factor-exposure analysis reveals no hidden factor bets ($|t| < 0.8$, $R^2 = 0.0003$). Notably, \textbf{dynamic stock selection + DFL hurts} (TE = 0.0346 vs.\ 0.0287, $6\times$ higher turnover), validating our detached selection design. A direct-weight MLP (no QP) achieves TE = 0.0622 ($2\times$ worse), confirming the QP is essential inductive bias.

Regarding computational cost, task-aware training converges in 15 epochs (26.3s per fold on Apple M1) vs.\ 47 epochs (38.9s) for MSE, because the task loss provides a more direct gradient signal. Grid search requires only 0.6s for 50 $\alpha$ values.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Practical guidelines.}
(1)~Always use a tracking-error-aligned objective---this is the single highest-impact change (5--12\% TE reduction).
(2)~Use grid search for 1-param models (0.6s); use DFL for 12+ param models.
(3)~Expect larger gains at lower $K/N$.
(4)~Detach stock selection from weight optimization.

\textbf{Decision rule:} $K/N > 0.3$: alignment gains minimal. 1-param: grid search on validation TE. 12+ params: DFL via differentiable QP.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Survivorship bias (current top-100 universe, not point-in-time); flat transaction cost model; richer models validated only on S\&P 500 (no DCC-GARCH comparison); 385-param conditional model overfits at $K{=}20$ (12-param structured model is the practical sweet spot).

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

We identified sparse index tracking as a setting where the predict-then-optimize gap is structurally amplified by the cardinality constraint, and provided a gradient alignment bound ($\cos \leq \sqrt{2K/N}$) that quantitatively predicts when task-aware estimation is worth the effort.

Our results decompose into two components. First, \textbf{objective alignment}: tuning the covariance model to minimize tracking error (even via grid search) accounts for the majority of the gain (5--12\% TE reduction, $p = 0.002$). This is the single most actionable finding---the Ledoit-Wolf $\alpha$ that minimizes Frobenius loss is not the one that minimizes tracking error. Second, \textbf{richer model training}: for models with 12+ parameters, differentiable optimization through the QP provides the only viable training path, achieving significant gains at $N{=}478$ ($p = 0.031$).

The improvement is robust across 19 years of data, nine rolling folds, three benchmarks, UCITS position limits, transaction costs, and turnover penalties---without increasing portfolio concentration. For practitioners, the takeaway is: \textbf{align your estimation objective with your decision objective, and use the simplest method that achieves alignment.}
